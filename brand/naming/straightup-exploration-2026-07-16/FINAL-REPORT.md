# The StraightUp Naming Exploration

**Prepared:** July 16, 2026  
**Category:** Premium, evidence-led skincare / healthy-aging skin care  
**Status:** Deep preliminary U.S. knockout and market-use screen—not a legal clearance opinion

## Executive decision

The best name to advance is **Sure Enough**.

It is the closest successor to StraightUp without imitating it. It is familiar, spoken rather than corporate, quietly defiant, and naturally confident. It also performed well in the preliminary screen: no live exact or edit-distance-one U.S. federal record surfaced, the expanded phonetic search found only an unrelated popcorn mark, and no exact skincare, beauty, wellness, supplement, or general commercial brand surfaced in the web queries run for this project.

The strongest attorney-clearance set is:

1. **Sure Enough**
2. **No Mystery**
3. **Plain to See**
4. **No Footnote**
5. **In Writing**
6. **Spelled Out**

**Show the Work** and **With Reason** are useful reserves. They fit the strategy, but they have less of the conversational attitude that made StraightUp compelling.

These are **preliminary-pass names**, not names that can responsibly be called finally “trademark cleared.” Final clearance requires counsel to assess the proposed goods and services, the full mark landscape, common-law use, priority markets, and likelihood of confusion.

## What StraightUp is really doing

StraightUp succeeds because it combines five things:

- familiar English rather than invented “beauty language”;
- a phrase people actually say;
- candid, no-spin attitude;
- forward energy rather than clinical caution;
- enough openness to become a company name, a voice, and a product system.

The right replacement is therefore not merely a synonym for “honest,” “transparent,” or “clear.” It needs to feel like a person looking you in the eye and standing behind what they say.

That distinction shaped the naming lanes:

- conversational conviction;
- plain speech and visibility;
- accountability and promises kept;
- evidence made visible;
- anti-fine-print / anti-hype language;
- intelligent judgment without institutional coldness.

| Matched federal case records reviewed by the automated screen | 7,520 |
| Initial exact/edit-distance-one provisional passes | 117 |
| Finalists receiving broader dominant/phrase/phonetic screening | 25 |
| Recommended attorney-clearance candidates | 6 |

The federal corpus was current through **July 6, 2026**. The core active classes examined were **003, 005, 010, 021, 035, and 044**, covering cosmetics/personal care, supplements and medicated products, medical devices, applicators/containers, retail, and beauty/skin services.

The first federal pass tested normalized exact matches and practical edit-distance-one variants. The finalist pass added dominant wording, collapsed phrase families, reversals, plurals, British spellings, and practical phonetic forms. That second pass materially changed the ranking.

## Ranked shortlist

### 1. Sure Enough — lead recommendation

**Why it works**

- Closest to StraightUp's conversational confidence.
- Familiar and human without sounding like generic “clean beauty.”
- Carries a satisfying sense of confirmation: the product did what the evidence said it would.
- Warm enough for a consumer brand and credible enough for an evidence-led system.
- Extensible beyond a single ingredient, benefit, age group, or product format.

**Screen result**

- No live exact U.S. federal hit found.
- No edit-distance-one federal hit found.
- Expanded phonetic screen found one unrelated SHO'NUFF popcorn mark; its broad Class 035 presence is not an exact category collision.
- No exact skincare, cosmetics, beauty, wellness, supplement, or general product/company brand surfaced in the web queries run.
- Music titles use the phrase, which is copyright/title noise rather than a direct skincare-brand conflict.

**Watch-out**

It is an ordinary phrase, so counsel should assess conceptual similarity and inherent strength, not just exact registry results. The voice must also avoid turning “sure enough” into an unsupported efficacy promise.

**In use**

- Sure Enough Barrier Support
- Sure Enough Peptide Serum
- “The claim. The evidence. Sure enough.”

### 2. No Mystery — strongest statement of the operating philosophy

**Why it works**

- Short, direct, and immediately legible.
- Captures the brand thesis: ingredients, evidence, choices, and limits are all explained.
- Has the same anti-obfuscation attitude as StraightUp without using the contested STRAIGHT element.
- Gives the brand a useful verbal platform: “No mystery ingredients. No mystery claims.”

**Screen result**

- No live exact or edit-distance-one federal hit found.
- No live relevant broader phrase-family record surfaced in the expanded screen.
- No exact relevant commercial brand surfaced in the web queries run.

**Watch-out**

“No mystery ingredients” and similar wording already appear descriptively in skincare and wellness copy. That weakens exclusivity even when no exact brand is present. It also leans slightly more functional than premium.

**In use**

- No Mystery Barrier Cream
- No Mystery Daily SPF
- “Every choice, explained.”

### 3. Plain to See — best skincare-specific double meaning

**Why it works**

- Connects transparent evidence with visible results.
- Familiar, calm, and intelligent.
- Feels human rather than clinical.
- Supports both education and performance without promising “younger.”

**Screen result**

- No live exact or edit-distance-one federal hit found.
- No live relevant broader phrase-family hit surfaced.
- No exact beauty or wellness brand surfaced in the web pass.

**Watch-out**

The phrase appears descriptively in aesthetics and skincare copy. That is not the same as an exact brand conflict, but it can reduce strength and search distinctiveness.

**In use**

- Plain to See Retinal Serum
- Plain to See Barrier Cream
- “What it does is plain to see.”

### 4. No Footnote — the sharpest anti-fine-print attitude

**Why it works**

- Compact, memorable, and culturally legible.
- Feels more ownable and less literal than “transparent skincare.”
- Creates a strong editorial device for packaging and evidence pages.
- Carries the defiance of StraightUp.

**Screen result**

- No live exact or edit-distance-one federal hit found.
- Broader federal search found only MORE THAN A FOOTNOTE! in Classes 035/041.
- No exact commercial brand surfaced in the web queries run.

**Watch-out**

INIKA Organic has used “no footnotes, no asterisks” descriptively in beauty copy. More importantly, the name must never mean “we have no qualifications.” The brand's actual promise is **no hidden qualifications**: limits and uncertainty should be made visible, not erased.

**In use**

- No Footnote Retinal
- No Footnote Daily Defense
- “No hidden qualifications.”

### 5. In Writing — strongest accountability signal

**Why it works**

- Suggests permanence, specificity, and a promise that can be checked.
- Premium and editorial without sounding faux-luxury.
- Naturally supports published formula rationales and evidence records.

**Screen result**

- No live exact or edit-distance-one federal hit found.
- No live relevant expanded phrase-family record surfaced.
- No exact beauty brand surfaced; Wellness in Writing is an expanded, adjacent wellness use rather than the exact proposed mark.

**Watch-out**

It is cooler and less energetic than StraightUp, and it is ordinary language. The brand expression would need warmth and bold typography to prevent it from feeling legalistic.

**In use**

- In Writing Barrier Repair
- In Writing Peptide Treatment
- “We put every decision in writing.”

### 6. Spelled Out — clearest education-and-transparency name

**Why it works**

- Directly supports the promise to explain formulas, evidence, and limitations.
- Familiar, spoken, and confident.
- Strong platform for ingredient and claim education.

**Screen result**

- No live exact or edit-distance-one federal hit found.
- No live relevant SPELL OUT / SPELL IT OUT phrase-family record surfaced in the expanded screen.
- No exact relevant commercial brand surfaced in the web pass.

**Watch-out**

It can sound instructional or mildly patronizing. The voice would need to say “we spell out our choices,” never “we spell skincare out for you.”

**In use**

- Spelled Out Barrier Serum
- Spelled Out Daily Moisture
- “Every choice, spelled out.”

## Controlled reserves

### Show the Work

This is the most exact expression of the locked brand thesis. It passed the exact, near, and broader federal screens. The weakness is ownership: it is a familiar schoolroom phrase and already appears in adjacent category copy, including skincare language. It is excellent as a platform line even if counsel advises against making it the masterbrand.

### With Reason

This passed the exact, near, and broader federal screens and no exact relevant brand surfaced. It is premium, intelligent, and calm. It simply lacks the snap and defiance of StraightUp; it feels like a reasoned editorial publication more than an insurgent consumer brand.

### As Shown

The only broader federal result was a false substring match. No exact relevant brand surfaced in the web pass. It is concise and visually confident, but extremely ordinary language creates weak searchability and likely narrow enforceability.

## Why “passed the screen” is not the same as “cleared”

The USPTO explains that likelihood of confusion can arise from similarity in **sound, appearance, meaning, or overall commercial impression**, and related goods do not need to share the same international class. Its recommended comprehensive search also extends beyond the federal database to common-law use, state registrations, and other sources. See the USPTO's guidance on [likelihood of confusion](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion), [federal searching](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching), and [comprehensive clearance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks).

This project includes:

- a large local U.S. federal knockout screen;
- a broader finalist similarity screen;
- targeted web/common-law discovery queries;
- strategic and verbal evaluation in the intended skincare category.

It does not include:

- an attorney's likelihood-of-confusion opinion;
- full goods-and-services analysis for every potentially similar mark;
- all state trademark and corporate-name registers;
- marketplace, distributor, app-store, and social-platform searches exhaustive enough for a legal opinion;
- candidate-level international clearance in every priority country;
- domain and handle availability confirmation.

WIPO's [Global Brand Database](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database) is useful for international discovery, but WIPO also advises searching national and regional registers before filing; see its [search-before-filing guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/how_to/search/index).

## Recommended next move

Send **Sure Enough, No Mystery, Plain to See, No Footnote, and In Writing** to trademark counsel as a ranked set, with **Sure Enough** clearly marked as the business preference.

Ask counsel to clear the planned launch and expansion scope, at minimum:

- Class 003: non-medicated skincare and cosmetics;
- Class 005: medicated skincare and ingestible expansion, if genuinely planned;
- Class 035: online retail and subscription/commerce services;
- Class 044: skin consultation, beauty, or treatment services, if planned;
- Classes 010 and 021 where devices, applicators, or tools are part of the roadmap.

Do not publish a winner, purchase packaging at scale, or announce the name until counsel has completed the comprehensive search and given a written risk assessment.
