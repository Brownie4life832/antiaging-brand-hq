# Independent naming handoff

> **Status:** Frozen after founder taste calibration on 2026-07-18.  
> **Purpose:** Brief an independent human naming lead without transmitting the old corpus, rankings, or failed selection bias.

## The assignment

Name a premium skincare masterbrand built on exceptional formulation science, transparent evidence and decision-making, and respect for women.

The customer can be confident in herself as she is and still want healthier, better-functioning, visibly improved skin. The brand must deliver performance without manufacturing shame, age panic, or inadequacy.

The name should create desire for the brand. It is **not required to encode or explain science, transparency, respect, confidence, or anti-aging**. Those values can live in the products, proof, voice, and experience.

## Desired response

Encountered cold, the name should feel:

- magnetic rather than merely sensible;
- modern, premium, and culturally alive;
- exciting and inspiring, with adult premium possibility rather than toy-like novelty;
- plausible on an exceptional skincare product;
- distinctive in sound, attitude, image, or idea;
- large enough to hold a multi-product brand world.

## Founder taste evidence

These are diagnostic references, not candidates or templates:

- **Straight Face:** strong because it has idiomatic snap, attitude, face relevance, and a real double meaning. It does not feel final.
- **Bare Facts:** nearly ideal mechanism because bareness/beauty and candor arrive together immediately. It is too close to an established skincare brand.
- **Fineprint:** intellectually interesting because it suggests ingredient and evidence transparency. It lacks emotional aspiration.
- **Goldseam:** has some appeal as a compact, visual brand object.
- **Byline:** has an interesting verbal and authorship quality, though its skincare story is not sufficient.

Do not infer that the answer must be another two-word idiom. The qualities matter more than the construction.

### Real-world taste controls

The founder loves or likes names as varied as `Liquid Death`, `Gruns`, `Drunk Elephant`, `Patagonia`, `Peloton`, `Uniqlo`, `Red Bull`, `Kleenex`, `Swiffer`, `Manscaped`, `native`, `Sunday Riley`, `OneSkin`, `The Ordinary`, `Proven`, `Glossier`, `Rhode`, `Naturium`, and `Biossance`.

The shared preference is not a grammatical form. It is a **complete, aspirational brand object** with a memorable verbal silhouette. A name may create a world, state a provocative premise, land as a decisive word, feel like a proper identity, connect directly to the category, or reward discovery through meaningful construction.

Key boundaries:

- `Supergoop!` has excitement but feels childish, uninspiring, and toy-like rather than premium.
- `Paula's Choice` feels like an ordinary name with a generic word attached: flat and unaspirational.
- `Biossance` became appealing once its `biology` + `renaissance` construction was recognized. Intrinsic one-line discovery can add real value; retrofitted strategic explanation cannot.

The full evidence and corrected rules are in [`12-founder-taste-calibration.md`](12-founder-taste-calibration.md).

## Automatic rejections

- a generic statement or two bland words paired together;
- a slogan masquerading as a masterbrand;
- a name whose skincare relevance exists only in its rationale;
- therapy, consultancy, publishing, education, or corporate language;
- an obscure term selected for an impressive explanation;
- an arbitrary faux-European luxury coinage;
- an awkward or low-energy `skin-` compound whose whole is no more compelling than its parts;
- childish, cutesy, toy-like, or novelty language that cannot support adult premium aspiration;
- a generic possessive or founder construction without a distinctive identity;
- tools, trades, measuring instruments, or industrial finishes;
- shame, fear, age denial, correction of the woman, or the claim that skincare use is inauthentic;
- anything that is admirable strategically but unwanted on a premium bottle.

## Founder taste calibration — completed

The founder evaluated eleven volunteered loved names and twenty controlled skincare names without logos, packaging, scores, or legal-status cues. The approved taste map is [`12-founder-taste-calibration.md`](12-founder-taste-calibration.md). It supersedes blanket bans on two-word names, `skin-` compounds, ordinary vocabulary, conceptual phrases, and coined words.

## Three calibrated creative worlds

### 1. Charged worlds

Concrete collisions, specific places, creatures, phenomena, rituals, materials, or other vivid sources that create an immediate brand universe. Energy must feel adult and inspiring, not cute or toy-like.

### 2. Meaningful inventions

Compact, pronounceable constructions with intrinsic logic, such as fused roots, purposeful transformations, or kinetic verbal objects. The construction may reward a second look, but it must be explainable in one line and embedded in the name itself.

### 3. Decisive identities

Confident standalone words, proper-name forms, conceptual stances, or clean category ideas that feel complete and aspirational. They must land with more authority than a generic statement, possessive, or slogan.

## Generation structure

Develop three genuinely distinct creative worlds. They should differ in source material, emotional atmosphere, and linguistic form—not just in strategic rationale.

The namer may generate extensively in private, but presents no more than five names from each world. The first presentation contains only the names in neutral type, randomized within each world.

Do not provide:

- logos or packaging;
- rationales, taglines, or moodboards;
- numeric scores or internal rankings;
- domain status;
- trademark badges;
- references to the 13,000-name archive.

Remove only obvious exact conflicts in skincare or beauty before presentation. Deeper trademark, linguistic, domain, and regulatory review begins after genuine founder pull exists.

## Founder evaluation sequence

### Pass 1 — naked desire

For every name, record only:

1. immediate association;
2. whether there is instinctive pull;
3. whether the founder wants to see it on a premium skincare bottle;
4. any interesting mechanism worth preserving even if the name fails.

No score is used. `No` is complete data.

### Pass 2 — spoken and remembered

Only names with real pull are spoken in simple contexts, such as “I use ___” and “This is from ___.” Test unaided recall after a delay.

### Pass 3 — meaning and stretch

Reveal the source and one-sentence story. A genuine construction embedded in the name may materially improve the reaction, as `Biossance` did when `biology` + `renaissance` became visible. Reject any explanation that requires a paragraph, imports unrelated values, or merely disguises a weak expression. Confirm that the resulting name can support the full product range.

### Pass 4 — clearance

Only then conduct structured trademark, common-law, domain, language, and cultural screening with qualified counsel where appropriate.

## Governing test

> Does the name create immediate pull—or contain an elegant intrinsic discovery that makes the founder want the brand to exist once seen?

If neither is true, strategic logic cannot promote it.
