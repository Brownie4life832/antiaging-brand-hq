# Founder diagnostic findings

> **Completed:** 2026-07-18  
> **Effect:** Freezes the creative controls and Cycle 1 approaches below.

## What the response established

The founder does not merely dislike specific words. The response reveals a consistent hierarchy:

1. **Meaning must arrive in one beat.** Unfamiliar real words and opaque inventions fail before their sound can help them.
2. **The skincare connection must be natural.** A name cannot depend on a strategy presentation to explain why it belongs to anti-aging or skin health.
3. **Energy is mandatory.** A comprehensible name can still fail if it is merely sensible, tasteful, or grammatically correct.
4. **Tone matters, but tone cannot replace meaning.** `Elvenne` had a pleasant tone but no useful idea; `Vernier` had some sound interest but insufficient relevance.
5. **A proposition can attract if it has a satisfying ring and a legible goal.** `Standard of One` had some appeal in form but did not explain what the standard was for.

## Five positive rules

1. **One-beat comprehension:** the source idea is recognizable without research.
2. **Natural skincare relevance:** face, skin health, time, improvement, science, or personal confidence connects immediately.
3. **Verbal voltage:** sound, compression, tension, or wordplay creates excitement beyond the literal meaning.
4. **Premium tone with purpose:** the name can sound elevated, but every elegant sound must carry an intelligible idea.
5. **A strong brand object:** the result should feel like a mark someone could desire, not a sentence someone could agree with.

## Five rejection rules

1. No unfamiliar dictionary, cultural, medical, or technical word whose meaning must be taught.
2. No arbitrary faux-house coinage that merely sounds expensive.
3. No generic statement, virtue phrase, or two-word combination with no surprising third meaning.
4. No forced `skin-` construction, awkward portmanteau, or accidental negative sound such as `Skinspect` → “suspect.”
5. No mundane trade, instrument, or industrial association that cheapens or derails premium skincare.

## Frozen Cycle 1 approaches

### A. Category idea with voltage

Directly relevant concepts involving face, skin, time, health, visible improvement, identity, and evidence—compressed through double meaning, contradiction, or a strong naming shape. This approach inherits the strategic relevance of `Made for This Face` without its sentence-like blandness.

### B. Legible invention

A familiar source word remains visible after one purposeful change, fusion, deletion, or re-segmentation. This is the founder's `Comfrt` preference applied to skin health, confidence, science, and improvement. No interchangeable Latinate endings.

### C. Charged real-world metaphor

Familiar real words or compounds whose connection to healthier skin, self-recognition, proof, or improvement is instantly imaginable. The metaphor must be common enough to understand and vivid enough to create a brand world. No obscure technical terms.

## Deliberate exclusions from Cycle 1

- Empty transparency slogans such as `Show the Work`.
- Unexplained scientific vocabulary such as `Allostasis`.
- Plumbing, measuring-tool, and workshop nouns.
- Editorial phrases that sound like content properties rather than skincare brands.
- Calm-but-dead generic English.
- Invented “luxury houses” with no semantic anchor.
