## Cycle 2 mechanism

Generate only names that satisfy all five conditions:

1. **Familiar on first hearing.** No dictionary lesson or fabricated luxury language.
2. **A genuine semantic hinge.** The expression must carry two useful meanings, not two words plus a rationale.
3. **Immediate face, skin, appearance, formulation, or skin-health relevance.** The category connection must be felt before explanation.
4. **Emotional lift.** The name should suggest confidence, momentum, vitality, self-possession, or optimism rather than merely ingredient disclosure.
5. **Logical integrity.** The brand may improve skin without implying that women are false, defective, or betraying themselves by using skincare.

## Exclusions

- The `bare + face/fact` sound field, because it risks confusion with established skincare use.
- Commands or idioms about age that can sound admonishing.
- No-retouch or no-change promises that contradict the product's purpose.
- Flat honesty statements, generic claims, and forced `skin-` compounds.
- Names that are emotionally positive but have no natural skincare reading.

## Cycle 2 stop rule

Only a small set that works naked and survives preliminary collision research reaches the founder. Availability cannot rescue a weak name. If the surviving mechanism yields no credible candidate, stop the round rather than reopen unrelated naming exercises.
