# Adaptive naming exploration protocol

> **Status:** Active. Supersedes the proposal to narrow around `Glass Orchard`, `Formistry`, or `Full Stop`.

## Why the process changed

Run 1 produced zero likes, three `interesting` reactions, one neutral, and eleven dislikes. An `interesting` reaction identifies a potentially useful ingredient in a name; it does not establish a territory. Narrowing at that point would turn weak signals into a creative constraint and repeat the earlier convergence problem.

## Objective

Continue broad exploration while making every next name informative. Names are presented one at a time, without rationale. The sequence deliberately changes linguistic form, tone, category distance, and type of meaning so the founder is never trapped inside a themed batch.

## Exploration dimensions

The sequence will move across:

### Word form

- familiar standalone word;
- specific real term;
- proper-name or place-like identity;
- purposeful invention;
- compound;
- established idiom or phrase.

### Category relationship

- direct skin or complexion relevance;
- formulation, laboratory, evidence, or craft relevance;
- beauty-adjacent material, light, surface, or transformation;
- world-bearing category distance.

### Emotional register

- adult wit;
- authority;
- aspiration;
- wonder;
- cultural attitude;
- premium restraint.

### Meaning arrival

- immediate comprehension;
- second-look discovery;
- primarily phonetic or identity-driven appeal.

## Adaptive selection rule

Each reaction changes the sampling weights, not the scope.

- `Like` increases the probability of testing both neighboring and deliberately contrasting forms.
- `Interesting` preserves the specific useful element but does not promote the whole mechanism.
- `Neutral` suggests acceptable sound with insufficient energy; the next related test must add a materially different idea.
- `Dislike` removes the precise failure—not the entire grammatical category—unless the same failure repeats across multiple clean tests.

No candidate is selected merely because it resembles a prior `interesting` name.

## Founder interaction

One name is shown per message. The founder responds naturally with like, dislike, neutral, interesting, or comments. Rationales remain hidden unless the founder asks for the intrinsic construction or the name survives the cold reaction.

No numeric scoring is used.

## Consolidation rule

Do not select a naming territory until either:

1. at least three names receive actual `like` reactions and a pattern survives comparison; or
2. thirty varied names complete the exploration cycle.

At that point, summarize observed preferences and contradictions. If no credible pull exists, change the creative source material or external creator—not merely the wording.

## Collision rule

Before presentation, remove obvious exact active skincare, cosmetics, beauty, or closely adjacent personal-care uses. This is only a preliminary knockout. Full clearance remains deferred until founder pull exists.

## Current queue design

The initial queue contains varied probes rather than a visible shortlist. It includes specific scientific terms, evidence language, craft identities, world-bearing nouns, adult idioms, category constructions, and longevity ideas. The order may change after every response to maximize new information.
