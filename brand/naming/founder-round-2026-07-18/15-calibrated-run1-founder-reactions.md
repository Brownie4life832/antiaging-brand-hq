## What the run disproved

### Semantic collision is not inherently exciting

`Silk Thunder`, `Moon Alloy`, `Private Weather`, and `Living Quartz` show that combining vivid nouns or opposing materials does not create a brand world by itself. The founder immediately notices when one word contributes no useful meaning or when the relationship is arbitrary.

This sharpens the `Liquid Death` and `Drunk Elephant` lesson: their appeal cannot be reproduced by pairing unrelated concrete words. The whole must create an immediately graspable image, premise, character, or story.

### Embedded logic is not sufficient for an invention

`Facience`, `Floradigm`, and `Ethicacy` had one-line constructions but still failed. Meaningful roots are valuable only when the resulting word has natural sound, immediate imaginative payoff, and the stature of a real brand.

`Formistry` survived because it did more than fuse roots: it immediately created a role and an enjoyable world—the customer can imagine formulation as a craft practiced in a lab.

### Decisiveness is not enough without a product idea

`Intact`, `Rare Form`, `Ground Truth`, and `Plain Sight` were rejected despite sounding confident or strategically relevant. `Full Stop` survived because the idiom could be applied directly to formulation superiority. Even then, its youthful tone may prevent premium aspiration.

## Three surviving mechanisms

### 1. Beautiful material with skin resonance

The live part of `Glass Orchard` is not arbitrary poetic contrast. It is glass as beauty, fragility, shine, translucence, and a possible visual analogy for skin. A future expression must make every component contribute.

### 2. Formulation as fascinating craft

`Formistry` reveals a promising brand world: serious laboratory formulation can feel inventive, skilled, and fun rather than sterile. The opportunity is not necessarily another `-istry` coinage; it is the imaginative identity of the formulator and the formula-making process.

### 3. Adult, definitive performance authority

`Full Stop` suggests an uncompromising conclusion: this is the best formulation, period. The mechanism has clarity and confidence, but the next expression must feel more adult, premium, and inspiring.

## Correct next move

Do not regenerate across all three old worlds. First determine which surviving mechanism has the strongest founder pull. Then create a small contrast set inside that mechanism and continue the one-name-at-a-time reaction format.
