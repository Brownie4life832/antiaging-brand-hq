# Reveal review brief

## The category

The names were generated blind. The actual assignment is a master brand for a modern skincare company.

## Brand behavior

- The company earns trust by staying close to the newest credible studies and adopting new technology when the evidence deserves it.
- It maintains a stable, high-performance core routine while keeping an advancing edge. Newness is useful only when it improves the standard.
- It offers a few excellent products, not an endless catalog.
- It explains ingredients and product choices plainly, answers questions through real expertise, and responds to customers' concerns.
- It rejects exaggerated claims, confusing pseudo-science, age shame, and marketing theater.
- The tone can be entertaining and provocative, but the substance must be rigorous.

## The three confidence layers

1. **Product confidence:** I believe this is genuinely good and worth using.
2. **Self-confidence:** I feel good about myself and about aging.
3. **Decision confidence:** I feel informed, intelligent, and in control.

Technology, biomechanics, and ingredient detail can live in the story; they do not need to appear literally in the name.

## Six strategic worlds

1. **Earned Confidence** — assurance with reasons; intelligent self-possession.
2. **The Long View** — a positive relationship with time, continuity, and aging.
3. **The Living Standard** — a trusted core that advances when science earns the change.
4. **Beauty for Skeptics** — wit, discernment, receipts, and resistance to hype.
5. **On Your Terms** — agency, personal authority, and control without correction or shame.
6. **Performance Without Theater** — fewer, better, high-utility products without spectacle.

## Review test

Review the naked name first, then allow one sentence of brand story. Keep only names that become a powerful and believable skincare idea after the reveal.

Score each retained name from **8.0 to 10.0** using:

- 25% strategic fit across the confidence layers
- 20% distinctiveness and surprise
- 15% processing fluency and verbal quality
- 15% stretch as a long-lived master brand
- 15% bottle, headline, and spoken-use fit
- 10% story ignition: the name starts a story instead of stating a fact

## Hard cuts

- Requires obscure etymology or a paragraph of explanation
- Sounds like a generic serum, clinic, supplement, SaaS tool, or ingredient vendor
- Repeats exhausted truth/proof/clarity language without a fresh turn
- Leans on age correction, youth worship, shame, or an exaggerated efficacy claim
- Falls into anonymous calm/repair/nature language or generic faux-Latin luxury
- Awkward pronunciation, spelling that must constantly be corrected, or weak bottle presence
- Works only as a campaign line and cannot carry a company and product system

Trademark status is a separate mechanical gate. Reviewers should flag obvious risk, but should not pretend to give legal clearance.
