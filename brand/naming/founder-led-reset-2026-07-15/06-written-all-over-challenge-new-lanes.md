# Written All Over Challenge: New-Lane Round

> **As of:** 2026-07-15  
> **Launch market:** United States  
> **Category:** Anti-aging skincare, cosmetics, related retail and education  
> **Status:** Preliminary knockout screening only. No name is legally cleared; a finalist still requires a comprehensive attorney search.

## Outcome

**No new name from this round earned the right to displace WRITTEN ALL OVER.**

That is not a claim that WRITTEN ALL OVER is perfect. It is a decision that none of the new directions produced a candidate with a better combined balance of brand character, skin relevance, strategic truth, and preliminary ownability.

WRITTEN ALL OVER remains the incumbent because it does two jobs without sounding engineered:

- It naturally completes as “written all over your face,” creating immediate but indirect category relevance.
- “Written” supports documentation, disclosure, substantiation, and education.
- It feels human and culturally familiar before it feels clinical or institutional.
- It contains enough tension and ambiguity to build a brand world around it.

Its liabilities remain real: it is a common phrase and may receive narrow protection; it can imply that age, fatigue, or damage is visibly “written all over” the customer's face; and exact digital availability remains unconfirmed. Those issues require counsel and careful positioning, not more creative rationalization.

## The new lanes explored

The prior round explored accountable promises, evidence traveling with the product, and ordinary language acquiring a facial or quantitative second meaning. This round moved outside those three territories.

### 1. Conservation and things that age well

Language from condition reports, archival care, maintenance, preservation, and stewardship. The strategic idea was to make anti-aging feel like intelligent long-term care rather than fear of age.

**Why the lane failed:** The strongest phrases were already used in skincare, sounded like package instructions, or became generic descriptions of product condition and aftercare.

### 2. Reliability, warranties, and performance culture

Language from durable goods, guarantees, service lives, endurance, and follow-through. The strategic idea was a formula that keeps performing rather than a brand making miraculous promises.

**Why the lane failed:** Warranty language felt transactional or fatalistic, and the strongest candidate collided with active LIFETIME-family marks in beauty, wellness, and cosmetic services. It also introduced warranty ambiguity that does not help a topical skincare brand.

### 3. Honest limits and radical candor

Language from disclosures, caveats, instructions, warnings, terms, and the willingness to say what a product cannot do.

**Why the lane failed:** The most truthful language behaved like safety copy, a website heading, or a legal disclaimer. In skincare, several phrases also primed irritation and risk before efficacy.

### 4. Customer comprehension and agency

Language from translation, legibility, understanding, judging for oneself, and making the science usable. This lane treated the customer as an intelligent participant rather than a passive believer.

**Why the lane failed:** The strongest constructions were ordinary calls to action rather than proprietary names. One otherwise credible double meaning turned out to be Canon's longstanding “See What We Mean” trademark and advertising slogan, documented in Canon's own 1999 guide.

### 5. Compounding value and the long game

Language from consistency, cumulative benefit, repeated practice, and value that becomes visible over time.

**Why the lane failed:** The most natural phrases were already products, brands, or highly descriptive skincare instructions. They tended to overpromise outcomes or sound like regimen copy.

### 6. Care labels and product-use systems

Language borrowed from garment care, handling, and use instructions. The strategic idea was to make education native to the package and turn proper use into a recognizable brand behavior.

**Why the lane failed:** This is a strong identity-system lane but a weak masterbrand lane. The language belongs on panels, inserts, routines, and education modules; as a company name it is likely to be perceived as informational matter rather than source identification.

## Finalist audit from the private generation set

The names below are recorded to prevent accidental recycling. They are not recommendations.

| Candidate | Decision | Decisive finding |
|---|---|---|
| **CARE INSTRUCTIONS** | **RED — masterbrand retire** | Ordinary instructional language, extensive skincare aftercare use, exact `.com` already registered, and substantial descriptiveness/failure-to-function risk. Potentially useful as an education-system label, not the house name. |
| **CHECK OUR WORK** | **RED — masterbrand retire** | Skinfix, Regimen Lab, and other beauty businesses already use the phrase as ordinary testing/transparency copy. It behaves like a manifesto or call to action, and the exact `.com` is occupied. |
| **LIMITED LIFETIME** | **RED — retire** | Active LIFETIME-family marks exist in cosmetic dermatology, supplements, spa services, and EU Class 3 beauty; beauty-device warranty use further weakens the territory. The meaning also introduces mortality and warranty baggage. |
| **LET IT SINK IN** | **YELLOW — withhold** | Excellent comprehension/absorption double meaning, but it is pervasive skincare application copy, the exact `.com` is registered, and a fresh SKININ Class 3 application creates additional watch risk. It is not a presentable pass. |
| **SEE WHAT WE MEAN** | **RED — commercial retire** | Canon expressly identifies the exact phrase as its trademark in company materials, and the phrase is also ordinary proof language across beauty. Even if a current Class 3 conflict were not found, it is neither clean nor ownable enough for this decision. |
| **PROCEED WITH CARE** | **RED — retire** | Ordinary safety-warning structure, exact use in skincare warnings about actives and irritation, international Class 3 “made/handle with care” crowding, occupied `.com`, and a danger-coded first impression. |

## Evidence highlights

- The USPTO warns that common informational wording may fail to function as a trademark and that descriptive wording may be difficult or impossible to register strongly. [USPTO common application problems](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/common-problems)
- Skinfix uses “How We Check Our Work” as an ordinary formulation/transparency heading, and Regimen Lab uses “Want to check our work?” alongside formula percentages. [Skinfix](https://skinfix.com/pages/how-we-formulate), [Regimen Lab](https://regimenlab.com/products/level-serum/Level%20Serum)
- Exact “proceed with care” use appears as skincare safety/instruction language around active ingredients. [Beauticate](https://www.beauticate.com/beauty-style/skin-care/how-to-get-the-most-out-of-active-skincare/), [ACURE](https://acure.com/blogs/acure/what-is-ferulic-acid)
- Canon's own BJC-8200 guide identifies “See What We Mean” as a Canon trademark. [Canon quick-start guide](https://gdlp01.c-wss.com/gds/8/0900007428/01/BJC-8200_Setup.pdf)
- Exact “let it sink in” use is already a skincare article concept describing product sequence and absorption. [PhycoHealth](https://www.phycohealth.com/blogs/product-stories-how-to-choose/let-it-sink-in-when-your-skincare-sequence-matters-and-why-it-s-never-one-size-fits-all), [Ohra](https://ohrabeauty.com/blogs/journal/let-it-sink-in-the-science-of-skincare-timing)
- Active U.S. LIFETIME/LIFE TIME marks exist in cosmetic dermatology, supplements, and spa services; a related LIFETIME longevity group filing covers Classes 3, 5, 35, and 44. [Class 44 cosmetic dermatology](https://trademarks.justia.com/765/30/lifetime-76530964.html), [Class 5 supplements](https://trademarks.justia.com/870/67/life-87067995.html), [multi-class longevity filing](https://trademarks.justia.com/793/67/lifetime-the-longevity-79367891.html)

## Why this round matters

The earlier process failed when strategic relevance was allowed to compensate for weak brand character. A name can accurately express transparency and still be bad because it sounds like a policy, heading, instruction, or campaign.

The correct test is multiplicative, not additive. A viable finalist must be:

1. A compelling brand before it is explained.
2. Sharper after the skincare context is revealed.
3. True to the operating model, not merely the launch copy.
4. Emotionally usable across products and years.
5. Free of an obvious legal, commercial, or cultural knockout.

If any one of those is near zero, the total is near zero. WRITTEN ALL OVER is the first name in this exercise to remain strong on all five at once.

## Decision recommendation for tomorrow

Stop broad creative generation and move **WRITTEN ALL OVER** into an expedited comprehensive attorney search. Keep **NO COVER STORY** and **WORKS CITED** as the two comparison controls, not as an expanding shortlist. If counsel finds a material conflict, reopen only the most structurally different naming lanes—coined/compressed words, borrowed expert vocabulary, and plainspoken category propositions—rather than generating more transparency idioms.
