# 250 additional net-new anti-aging brand names — wave 2

> **Scope:** These names were developed after the first 250 and checked by normalized exact matching against an expanded retained corpus of **2,345 names**: 2,095 historical candidates plus all 250 first-wave names. Normalization removes case, punctuation, spacing, apostrophes, and diacritics.
>
> **Score meaning:** Creative-fit score for this specific brand, before trademark, common-law, domain, social, linguistic, or regulatory clearance. Every score is strictly above 8.0/10. A high score is not an availability claim.

## Final cut

Wave 1 found category-readable intelligence. Wave 2's strongest discovery is a more human tension: **beauty desire without beauty gullibility**. The best names let the customer want visible improvement while sounding adult, curious, self-aware, and hard to fool.

## What changed in this wave

More than 1,000 raw and intermediate possibilities were generated across three independent passes. This wave deliberately reduced `skin + noun`, proof/editorial, and stylized missing-vowel families from wave 1. It expanded into productive contradiction, adult/pro-age language, participatory transparency, optics, industrial design, accounting, governance, signal processing, customer tribes, and spoken sound patterns.

The sound pass treated names as things people say, not only things they see, but it was a creative generation lens rather than a documented oral test. Research suggests phonetic repetition can improve affect and that individual phonemes can shape perceived meaning; those effects are tendencies rather than substitutes for respondent testing. Still pending on any shortlist: five-second category recognition, hear-to-spell and delayed recall, semantic differentials, native-speaker review, and live confusing-similarity clearance.

## Screening note

All 250 passed normalized retained-corpus exact deduplication, internal deduplication, and a manual brief-fit review. Pronunciation, product syntax, and obvious-association concerns were considered as editorial filters, but the longlist intentionally retains some experimental constructions that need respondent testing. A dated exact-web knockout removed several direct current conflicts from the leading group; it was not a comprehensive similarity search. This is **not** live trademark or common-law clearance.

Under USPTO guidance, real clearance must test similarity in sound, appearance, meaning, and overall commercial impression for related goods—not merely exact matches. The strongest candidates should next receive a documented knockout across Class 3 and relevant adjacent classes, internet/common-law use, WIPO and regional sources, domains, handles, and native-speaker linguistic review before counsel evaluates the final 3–5.
