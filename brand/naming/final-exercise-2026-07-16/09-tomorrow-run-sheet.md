# Decision-day run sheet — Thursday, 2026-07-16

> **Hard stop:** one preferred master brand and one automatic legal fallback recorded by **6:00 p.m. America/New_York**.

## Tonight — 30 minutes

1. Recruit **six people** for 12–15 minute individual sessions. At least four should be 35–55 and personally buy premium skincare; the remaining two can be adjacent buyers. Do not recruit naming or branding professionals unless they are also genuine category buyers.
2. Tell them only: “I am testing how six possible skincare names communicate. This is not a logo or preference test.” Do not send the names in advance.
3. Prepare six plain cards using one neutral sans-serif, black on white, same type size:
   - Restraint
   - Bearing
   - Leeway
   - In Earnest
   - Byline
   - Actual
4. Prepare six identical front panels by replacing only the first line in this template:

   ```text
   RESTRAINT
   Barrier Repair Cream
   Ceramides + Peptides
   50 mL / 1.7 fl oz
   ```

5. Read the frozen brief once. Do not browse for, generate, or accept another name.

If six buyers are impossible, use three genuine target buyers. If nobody is available, skip fake research and use the current evidence: **Restraint primary, Bearing fallback**.

## Split the usability test

Do not let seeing the spelling invalidate a hear-to-spell test.

- **Respondents 1, 3, and 5 — audio first:** say each name once without showing it. Ask them to repeat it and spell what they would search. Then show the naked-name card.
- **Respondents 2, 4, and 6 — visual first:** show each naked-name card silently. Ask them to say it aloud. Do not count a later spelling answer from these respondents as hear-to-spell evidence.

After the first modality pass, all six respondents see the identical skincare prototype.

## Exact interview script

For each naked name, ask in this order:

1. “What are the first three things this makes you think of?”
2. “What kind of company do you expect this to be?”
3. “What price level and personality do you expect?”
4. “What, if anything, feels wrong, negative, cheap, juvenile, medical, or confusing?”
5. “What would you expect this company to promise or be especially good at?”

Then show the identical skincare front panel and ask:

6. “Now that you know it is premium skincare, what changes?”
7. “Imagine a new premium competitor launched tomorrow under this name. Does it sound credible, forgettable, concerning, or compelling—and why?”
8. “Would saying ‘I use [NAME]’ feel natural? Please say it aloud.”

Do **not** explain the intended story, defend a name, mention the founder’s favorites, or ask “Which one do you like?”

After all six, spend 10 minutes on an unrelated topic. Then ask:

9. “Write down every name you remember. Please do not guess from a list.”
10. “Which one would you be most curious to investigate at an $80–$100 price point? What specifically created that expectation?”

The second question is diagnostic, not a vote.

## Fatal gates at 1:30 p.m.

Eliminate rather than average away any of these:

- **Hear-to-spell failure:** two of the three audio-first respondents cannot recover a searchable spelling.
- **See-to-say failure:** two of the three visual-first respondents independently mispronounce it.
- **Repeated harm:** at least two respondents independently reach the same damaging association and it persists after the skincare prototype.
- **Category rejection:** at least three respondents still find it fundamentally wrong for premium skincare after seeing the neutral prototype.
- **Explanation dependence:** the name needs the intended story before it becomes credible.
- **Collision:** new legal/common-law evidence changes its preliminary status to red.

Do not eliminate merely because a participant personally dislikes a name. Do not retain a name because one participant loves it.

### The Restraint-specific gate

Ask no leading question about deprivation or coercion. If those meanings arise independently, record them. Restraint loses the primary position only if at least two target buyers reach the same punitive interpretation **and still reject it after learning that the restraint applies to formulas, steps, claims, and commercial excess—not to the person**.

### The Bearing-specific gate

Bearing loses the fallback position if mechanical/industrial meaning dominates for at least three respondents after the skincare prototype, or if professional clearance makes the pet/personal-care proximity imprudent.

## Final three at 2:00 p.m.

For each survivor, write exactly five lines:

1. Strongest observed commercial advantage.
2. Most serious observed weakness.
3. Exact preliminary clearance status.
4. What cold buyers actually inferred.
5. 2029 premortem: the likeliest way the name becomes limiting, embarrassing, forgettable, or expensive.

No new rationale or etymology may be introduced.

## Pairwise decision at 3:00 p.m.

Compare the final three head-to-head rather than awarding vague decimal scores. For each pair, select a winner on:

- **Distinctiveness and delayed memory — 40%**
- **Strategic leverage — 35%**
- **Fluency and verbal usability — 25%**

Use observed evidence. Clearance remains a gate, not points.

If the comparisons form a cycle, use this binding sequence:

1. Remove any fatal-gate failure.
2. Prefer lower known legal/common-law risk.
3. Prefer stronger unaided recall.
4. Prefer stronger hear-to-spell and see-to-say results.
5. Prefer the name whose loss to a competitor would create greater genuine regret.
6. If still tied, flip a coin. Do not invent precision the evidence does not contain.

## Founder decision at 5:30 p.m.

The founder writes privately, before hearing any final adviser argument:

- “The name I will build for ten years is ___.”
- “The automatic fallback if professional clearance fails is ___.”
- “It won because ___, ___, and ___.”
- “The weakness I accept knowingly is ___.”
- “The strategic promise I will protect is ___.”

At **6:00 p.m.**, sign and date the decision record. Freeze the selected name for 30 days. Only a new trademark, linguistic, or reputational fact can activate the fallback; anxiety, late taste, or a new name suggestion cannot reopen the process.

## Before public use

Send the preferred name and fallback to a U.S.-licensed trademark attorney for a comprehensive search covering federal records, common-law use, state registries, relevant domains, and intended international markets. At minimum discuss Classes 3, 5, 35, and 44. The decision tomorrow is a binding business preference with legal insurance—not a representation that either name is cleared.
