# Binding decision protocol — 2026-07-16

## Decision charter

- The founder is the single decision owner.
- One primary name will be selected by **6:00 p.m.**
- One ordered fallback will be recorded for legal insurance; it is not a co-winner.
- No new candidates enter after the generation cutoff.
- A rejected candidate returns only if the rejection was based on a demonstrable factual error.
- Taste, anxiety, a late opinion, or failure to feel an instant “aha” cannot reopen the process.
- After selection, the name is frozen for 30 days unless a new trademark, linguistic, or reputational fact triggers the automatic fallback.

## Stage 1 — Generate and mechanically clean (complete before decision day)

1. Run independent generation labs against the final brief.
2. Target roughly 900–1,200 fresh candidates; breadth comes from distinct briefs, not interchangeable suffixes.
3. Remove exact duplicates, prior-corpus matches, banned roots/forms, obvious competitor echoes, and names without one stable pronunciation.
4. Randomize the survivors and hide territory, source, and rationale.

## Stage 2 — Blind curation to 48

Use at least three independent passes. Each pass sees only the naked names and may advance no more than 10%.

Prompt:

> What credible commercial advantage could this naked name give this specific brand?

Advance by pairwise preference, not decimal scores. Preserve disagreement, but do not rescue a name with a story.

## Stage 3 — Fatal gates to 12

A single fatal failure eliminates the name:

- **Hear → spell:** a listener cannot recover a searchable spelling after hearing it once.
- **See → say:** there is no obvious standard pronunciation.
- **Cold meaning:** it produces a common damaging, juvenile, medical, cheap, or category-wrong association.
- **Explanation dependence:** the name is weak until its etymology is explained.
- **Competitor confusion:** it resembles a beauty, skincare, wellness, or longevity mark in sound, appearance, meaning, or commercial impression.
- **Claim risk:** it implies a medical effect or unsupported efficacy claim.
- **Headroom failure:** it traps the brand in one ingredient, product, technology, age stance, or benefit.
- **Preliminary collision:** meaningful federal, common-law, retail, domain, or social evidence makes pursuit imprudent.

The 12 survivors receive a one-sentence story only after passing as naked names. If the story needs more than 15 seconds, cut the name.

## Stage 4 — Six identical prototypes

Reduce to six and show each in exactly the same neutral system:

- `[NAME] Barrier Repair Cream`
- `[NAME] Renewal Serum`
- `[NAME] Daily SPF`
- “I use [NAME].”
- “Welcome to [NAME].”
- “[NAME] is an evidence-led skincare company.”
- A jar front, homepage masthead, search result, and spoken podcast introduction.

Do not give a weak name better design or copy.

## Stage 5 — Directional buyer test

Use 5–8 people resembling the intended buyer. More rushed respondents are less valuable than fewer attentive ones.

Randomize order and do not ask “Which do you like?” Ask each person to:

1. Say the name aloud.
2. Spell it after hearing it.
3. Give the first association.
4. Guess the category, price level, and personality.
5. Explain what the name makes them expect.
6. Recall names after a 10-minute unrelated task.
7. React to: “A new premium skincare competitor just launched called ___.”

Cut a name if pronunciation/search recovery is consistently poor or if multiple people independently reach the same damaging association. Preference alone does not select the winner.

## Stage 6 — Final three

For each finalist, document:

- Strongest strategic advantage.
- Most serious weakness.
- Preliminary clearance status: green / amber / red.
- What a cold buyer actually inferred.
- Unaided recall and verbal handoff.
- A 2029 failure premortem: how this name could become limiting, embarrassing, forgettable, or expensive.

Then run a pairwise bracket using only:

1. **Distinctiveness and memory — 40%**
2. **Strategic leverage — 35%**
3. **Fluency and verbal usability — 25%**

Clearance remains a gate, not points that can be averaged away.

## Binding tie-break sequence

1. Remove any fatal-gate failure.
2. Prefer lower known legal/common-law risk.
3. Prefer stronger observed unaided recall.
4. Prefer stronger hear-to-spell and see-to-say performance.
5. Prefer the name whose loss to a competitor would create greater genuine regret.
6. If still tied, flip a coin. The evidence has declared the names functionally equivalent; do not manufacture false precision.

If no candidate feels excellent, the highest surviving candidate still wins. “None” is not an option after Stage 3.

## What is recorded at 6:00 p.m.

- Selected master brand.
- Ordered legal fallback.
- Frozen strategic assumption.
- Three concrete reasons the name won.
- Principal weakness accepted knowingly.
- Preliminary clearance findings and required attorney follow-up.
- The 30-day no-reopen rule.

The USPTO recommends a comprehensive clearance search covering federal records and internet/common-law use, and evaluates similarity in sound, appearance, meaning, and overall commercial impression—not just exact matches. Preliminary screening is therefore triage, not legal clearance. Do not print packaging or launch publicly before professional review.
